Note on "AI" claims

Every vendor now markets AI. What actually matters for your goal: (1) fewer false positives via validated/proof-based findings, (2) API & GraphQL schema understanding, and (3) usable remediation guidance. Escape, Bright and StackHawk are genuinely API/AI-native; Invicti, Acunetix and HCL bolt strong AI onto mature engines. Judge on the FP rate and GraphQL depth in a proof-of-value, not the marketing.

Wrong lane for WAS

Keep it — but as network/host CVE scanning, not web-app DAST. It scans servers, services and ports against a 185k+ CVE feed. It won't crawl your apps, follow auth flows, or test your GraphQL/REST logic the way WAS did. Community Edition is free; Greenbone Enterprise appliances start around $5k/yr.

FITInfra vulnerability hygiene alongside — not instead of — a real DAST.

Recommendation

Primary recommendation

Lead with Invicti, layer Escape for APIs

Invicti is the cleanest upgrade from Nessus WAS for automated web-asset scanning: proof-based scanning removes the false-positive triage that quietly kills most scanning programmes, it covers REST/SOAP/gRPC/GraphQL and SPAs, scales across a large app portfolio with RBAC, and — critically for a security-conscious shop — runs on-prem or hybrid, not cloud-only.

Because your architecture is moving toward Apollo Router / GraphQL federation behind APISIX, add Escape as the API/GraphQL-security layer (or shortlist it head-to-head with Invicti's API module in your proof-of-value). GraphQL introspection abuse and business-logic flaws (BOLA/IDOR) are exactly where a generic DAST is weakest and where Escape is strongest.

Design the programme, not just the purchase

A tool is one layer. No DAST reliably catches business-logic flaws, complex authorization bugs, or race conditions — that gap is closed by people, not scanners. Build the buy into a repeatable programme:

LAYER 01

Automated DAST engine

Invicti (or your chosen leader) as the always-on scanner. Continuous/weekly for internet-facing assets; configure authenticated scans through Keycloak (OIDC) so it reaches protected areas, not just the login page.

LAYER 02

API & GraphQL testing

Feed OpenAPI / GraphQL schemas to the scanner (or Escape) so coverage follows your APISIX-fronted, federated graph — not just the HTML surface. This is where your roadmap concentrates risk.

LAYER 03

Shift-left in CI/CD

Wire scans into pipelines (native for StackHawk/Escape/Bright; container/CLI for Invicti/Burp EE) to catch regressions per-release. Gate builds on new criticals, not on the full backlog.

LAYER 04

Human validation

Use Core Impact or an annual third-party pentest (Burp Pro in expert hands) to validate exploitability and hunt logic/authz flaws. Keep OpenVAS for infra CVE hygiene. Scanners find; humans confirm.

For your environment specifically: validate three things in any proof-of-value before signing — (1) authenticated scanning works cleanly against a Keycloak/OIDC flow; (2) the scanner ingests your GraphQL schema and finds introspection/BOLA issues behind APISIX; (3) it deploys where your policy needs it (on-prem/hybrid for internal K8s services). Run the top two candidates against the same two real apps and compare confirmed findings vs. false positives — that head-to-head, not the datasheet, is what should decide it.
